# Annual Report Analysis

## Overview

This skill guides the analysis of uploaded corporate documents—annual reports, quarterly results, investor presentations, and conference call transcripts—for Indian listed companies.

## Key Sections to Extract

### Annual Report Structure (typical Indian company)
1. **Chairman/MD Letter**: Strategic direction, management commentary on performance.
2. **Management Discussion & Analysis (MD&A)**: Detailed segment analysis, risk factors, outlook.
3. **Financial Statements**: Standalone and consolidated P&L, Balance Sheet, Cash Flow.
4. **Notes to Accounts**: Accounting policies, contingent liabilities, related-party transactions.
5. **Auditor's Report**: Qualifications, emphasis of matter, key audit matters.
6. **Corporate Governance Report**: Board composition, committee details, promoter remuneration.
7. **Shareholding Pattern**: Quarter-on-quarter movement, top holders.
8. **ESG / CSR Section**: CSR spend, sustainability initiatives (mandatory ≥2% of avg PAT).

### Quarterly Results (BSE/NSE format)
- Revenue from operations (standalone and consolidated)
- EBITDA and margins (calculate if not stated)
- Exceptional items and their nature
- Segment-wise revenue and profit
- Order book / pipeline updates (where applicable)

## Extraction Strategy

### Step 1: Identify Document Type
Determine if the document is an annual report, quarterly result, investor presentation, or transcript. This affects which sections to prioritise.

### Step 2: Financial Data Extraction
- Look for comparative figures (current period vs prior period vs prior year).
- Identify restated figures if accounting policy changed.
- Note the basis: standalone (single entity) vs consolidated (with subsidiaries).

### Step 3: Qualitative Insights
- Management guidance on revenue/margin targets.
- Capex plans and funding sources.
- Segment commentary and geographic expansion.
- Risk factors the company itself highlights.

### Step 4: Cross-reference
- Compare management claims with actual financials.
- Check if guidance from previous reports was met.
- Look for consistency between MD&A narrative and numbers.

## Common Pitfalls

- Confusing standalone and consolidated numbers.
- Missing exceptional items that inflate/deflate PAT.
- Ignoring "Notes to Accounts" where material disclosures hide.
- Taking management guidance at face value without historical track record check.
- Not adjusting for stock splits or bonus issues when comparing EPS across years.

## Citation Guidelines

When referencing findings from the document:
- Always cite the page number where the data point was found.
- Quote specific figures with their exact context from the document.
- Distinguish between audited and unaudited figures.
- Note whether figures are standalone or consolidated.
